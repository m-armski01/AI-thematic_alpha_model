# thematic-alpha

**Does an active, macro-aware trading overlay beat simply holding a 12-stock AI / datacenter
basket?** A single-investor research study, net of costs, point-in-time, preregistered and
reproducible from public data.

The twelve names in [`data/reference/universe.csv`](data/reference/universe.csv) are the
author's own holdings, bought at the start of 2026. The basket is a given, not a selection
result, so the only benchmark that matters is **equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same basket**.
A point-in-time universe of 17 ETFs is the generalisation control. **What this is not**: an
institutional alpha strategy, a live trading system, or a claim about AI stocks in general.

> **Research and analysis system, not a live trading system.** It never places orders,
> integrates with a broker, or paper-trades. Historical results do not indicate future
> performance. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Results at a glance

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the overlay beat holding the basket, 2015–2026? | **No.** Slightly lower return and Sharpe, a shallower drawdown and lower beta: roughly break-even on a risk-adjusted basis |
| Did it beat holding the basket in 2026 year-to-date? | More return, at a similar beta but with higher volatility and a deeper drawdown. Eight months is too short to conclude |
| Does it help in the macro-stress regimes it was built for? | **Mixed.** It wins in rate shocks and elevated-VIX periods, and loses in VIX stress (> 25) by blocking re-entry |
| Do the rules generalise to an unbiased universe? | **No.** On the point-in-time ETF control the overlay loses to buy-and-hold on every headline metric |
| Where does the basket's return come from? | The names, not the rules: buy-and-hold alone shows the same 40% vs 13.5% gap between basket and ETFs |

## Headline: overlay vs buy-and-hold of the basket (2015-01-02 → 2026-09-11, net of costs, EUR)

![equity curves](outputs/base/figures/equity_curves.png)

| | Overlay | **Equal-weight buy-and-hold (same 12 names)** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total return | 4,942% | **5,149%** |
| CAGR | 39.9% | **40.4%** |
| Annualised volatility | 32.7% | 30.9% |
| Sharpe (excess over 3m T-bill) | 1.13 | **1.19** |
| Sortino (MAR 0) | 1.67 | 1.72 |
| Max drawdown | **−42.3%** | −45.0% |
| Calmar | 0.94 | 0.90 |
| Beta vs S&P 500 | 1.17 | 1.30 |
| Annualised turnover | 4.1× | 0.9× |

**Verdict** (generated by the report from the numbers). Over 140 months the overlay's CAGR is
lower than equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same basket (39.9% vs 40.4%), its Sharpe is lower
(1.13 vs 1.19), and its maximum drawdown is shallower (−42.3% vs −45.0%), at a lower realised
beta (1.17 vs 1.30). **The overlay is roughly break-even to modestly value-additive on a
risk-adjusted basis**: it gives up return relative to holding the basket in exchange for a
shallower drawdown. Idle cash earns the 3-month T-bill rate in every run, so holding cash is not
penalised.

The basket's 40% a year reflects the author's own selection and was not achievable ex-ante.
Every overlay figure is read against the buy-and-hold column, not against the S&P 500, which
made 14.1% a year over the window. Full report, with VaR, drawdown episodes, FX decomposition
and the market-context appendix: [`outputs/base/report.md`](outputs/base/report.md).

### 2026 year-to-date: the window the overlay was actually run

| 2026-01-01 → 2026-09-11 (8 months) | Overlay | Equal-weight buy-and-hold | S&P 500 (SPY) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total return | 177.9% | 84.9% | 12.8% |
| Sharpe (annualised) | 2.72 | 2.10 | 1.15 |
| Max drawdown | −32.2% | −28.5% | −7.5% |
| Beta vs S&P 500 | 2.27 | 2.23 | 0.99 |
| Annualised volatility | 59.7% | 45.7% | 12.7% |

The overlay added return at a similar beta (2.27 vs 2.23), with much higher volatility and a
deeper drawdown: it held five names instead of twelve, concentrated in the year's strongest
movers. CAGR, Calmar and alpha are suppressed on windows under 24 months, and eight months is
too short to conclude either way. Report: [`outputs/base_2026ytd/report.md`](outputs/base_2026ytd/report.md).

### Does the overlay help where it was designed to? Performance by regime

Every session is tagged by the state the gate could see that day: lagged VIX, the applied gate
state, and the 21-day change in the 10-year yield. The overlay is then compared with buy-and-hold
inside each bucket (basket, 2015–2026):

| Regime | Share of sessions | Overlay total return | Buy-and-hold total return | Worst drawdown (overlay / B&H) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| VIX calm (< 20) | 70.6% | 1,034% | 1,039% | −39.3% / −33.1% |
| VIX elevated (20–25) | 16.1% | 157% | 78% | −18.5% / −33.7% |
| VIX stress (> 25) | 13.2% | 73% | 159% | −27.2% / −26.7% |
| Gate risk-off | 28.3% | 224% | 266% | −26.3% / −28.5% |
| Rate shock (10y +40 bp / 21d) | 5.6% | −8.3% | −13.8% | −20.2% / −27.4% |

**The regime evidence is mixed.** The overlay beats holding the basket in one of the three
stress buckets (rate shocks). It trails in VIX stress and while the gate is risk-off, because
blocking re-entry above a VIX of 25 costs more recovery upside than it saves in drawdown. Its
edge sits in the *elevated* regime.

## Does this generalise? The point-in-time ETF control

The identical rules run on 17 sector, thematic and defensive ETFs defined point-in-time. A fund
enters only once it has 252 sessions of history and clears a 5 M EUR liquidity screen.

![ETF equity curves](outputs/study/etf_equity_curves.png)

| 2015–2026, net of costs | Overlay | S&P 500 (SPY) B&H | **Equal-weight B&H (same 17 ETFs)** | Naive momentum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CAGR | 5.9% | 14.1% | **13.5%** | 10.0% |
| Sharpe | 0.31 | 0.69 | **0.71** | 0.52 |
| Max drawdown | −30.5% | −33.5% | **−29.9%** | −27.0% |
| Annualised turnover | 9.8× | 0.1× | 0.2× | 23.0× |

On the control the overlay loses to holding the basket on every headline metric.

**Selection, measured.** The same rules, window and costs give 39.9% CAGR on the owned basket
and 5.9% on the ETF control (Sharpe 1.13 vs 0.31). Equal-weight buy-and-hold shows the same gap
with no rules at all (40.4% vs 13.5%). The return came from the basket, not the rules.

Reports: [`outputs/etf/report.md`](outputs/etf/report.md) and the 2026 YTD control
[`outputs/etf_2026ytd/report.md`](outputs/etf_2026ytd/report.md), where the overlay made +16.5%
against +11.0% for buy-and-hold with a deeper drawdown. Full numbers and the ablation of every
rule: [`outputs/study.md`](outputs/study.md).

## How the overlay works

- **Ranker.** Weekly cross-sectional z-score of 63-day momentum. It holds the top 5 equal-weight,
  and a rank buffer keeps a name until it falls below rank 8.
- **Macro gate.** Schmitt-trigger sub-gates on lagged VIX, the 10-year yield and oil, evaluated
  monthly. While any is engaged, the overlay makes no new entries or increases, and exits still
  follow the ranker.
- **Earnings mask.** No increase within 3 sessions before a known earnings date (basket only).
- **Execution.** Signal at the Friday close, trade at the next open, with 5 bp per side plus
  3 bp slippage. Idle cash earns the T-bill rate. Positions are capped at 35%, and a 2 pp
  no-trade band applies.
- **Discipline.** Every parameter was set from standard practice and preregistered in
  [`docs/preregistration_v2.md`](docs/preregistration_v2.md) before the runs that use it. Tests
  assert that no feature, target or trade can see the future.

The full rule table, turnover decomposition, ablation, literature context and data-problem log
are in [`docs/methodology.md`](docs/methodology.md).

## Limitations (read these first)

1. **Single personal basket.** The result is a statement about these twelve names and these
   rules, not about AI stocks or momentum overlays in general. The ETF control is the only
   generalisation check, and it says the rules do not travel.
2. **The basket's absolute returns carry its own selection.** The names were bought in 2026 as
   the AI-infrastructure winners. The 2015–2026 window is a *what-if* on holding them, used
   only relative to their own equal-weight buy-and-hold.
3. **Flat slippage.** 5 bp per side plus 3 bp slippage on every name regardless of size is
   optimistic for the smaller names (NBIS, IREN, LITE, COHR) and for re-entries after a gate
   release.
4. **Short live window.** The overlay was actually run for eight months of 2026. Annualised
   statistics are suppressed on that window and no verdict is drawn from it.
5. **No walk-forward.** Every parameter was preregistered, but there is no out-of-sample split.
   The ablation and the two sensitivity sets (softmax temperature, momentum window) in
   `outputs/study.md` are the only robustness evidence.
6. **The block-mode gate emptied the ETF book in 2020–21.** After the COVID crash the VIX stayed
   above the 20-point release level for a year and the gate blocked every re-entry. The ETF book
   was 6–20% invested from Q3 2020 to Q1 2021. With the gate disabled the ETF overlay makes 9.9%
   instead of 5.9%. On the basket the gate is neutral (Sharpe 1.13 either way).
7. **Residual selection bias in the ETF control.** The list holds only funds that still trade in
   2026. Liquidated or merged funds in the same categories are absent.
8. **The liquidity screen changes the benchmark.** At 5 M EUR of 21-day traded value the screen
   toggles COHR and IREN in and out of the basket, and buy-and-hold re-equalizes on every
   change. The screen-off ablation row shows the effect (overlay 43.7% vs 39.9% CAGR). The
   screen was not changed after the run.
9. **One dominant regime.** Eleven years of a mostly secular bull market with three drawdowns
   (2018, 2020, 2022). Sharpe ratios on a concentrated long-only basket are a regime statement,
   not a strategy statement.
10. **Unhedged FX.** The base currency is EUR and positions are in USD and KRW. The FX
    contribution is reported separately in each report.
11. **Macro data is current-vintage.** FRED series are revised, and the one-session publication
    lag is the only point-in-time control.
12. **Earnings dates** come from yfinance and were reviewed by hand. A ticker with none fails
    open. The ETF control has the mask disabled because ETFs report no earnings.

## Quickstart

Requires Python 3.11 or later and network access on the first run. Prices come from Yahoo
Finance (yfinance) and macro series from FRED's keyless CSV endpoint. No API keys are needed.
Downloads are cached as Parquet under `data/cache/`, which is gitignored; the repository ships
no market data.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/m-armski01/thematic_alpha_model.git
cd thematic_alpha_model
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e ".[dev]"

# Offline test suite: synthetic markets, golden regression, no-lookahead invariants
pytest

# Headline run: downloads data on first use, then writes outputs/base/
python -m thematic_alpha.run --config configs/base.yaml

# The control and the two 2026 year-to-date runs
python -m thematic_alpha.run --config configs/etf.yaml
python -m thematic_alpha.run --config configs/base_2026ytd.yaml
python -m thematic_alpha.run --config configs/etf_2026ytd.yaml

# The study: both universes, ablation and sensitivity sets -> outputs/study.md
python -m thematic_alpha.study --configs configs/etf.yaml configs/base.yaml
```

**Reproducibility.** Every config pins `run.end_date: 2026-09-11`, the snapshot behind the
committed reports. Reruns on the same cached data are byte-identical. A fresh download will
differ slightly from the committed numbers, because Yahoo re-adjusts historical closes for
dividends and splits paid after the snapshot and FRED revises its series. To study a later
window, raise `end_date` in the config and pass `--refresh`.
